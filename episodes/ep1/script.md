## CHAPTER 1 — YOUR MAP IS LYING TO YOU

> Let's start with the map in your head, because it's probably wrong.

**Fact 1 — Reno vs Los Angeles**
> Number one. Reno, Nevada is farther west than Los Angeles. California's coast curves east as it heads south, so L.A. ends up to the east of a desert city in Nevada. Draw a line straight south from Reno and it misses Los Angeles completely, passing near Santa Barbara before running into the Pacific.

**[Visual]** Fly to California; longitude lines draw down from Reno and L.A.; coastline highlights curving east.

**Fact 2 — South America is east of Florida**
> Number two. Almost all of South America is east of Miami. If you flew straight south from Florida, you'd miss most of the continent and end up over the Pacific Ocean. Lima, the capital of Peru, is about three degrees east of Miami, and even Santiago in Chile, on the west coast, is east of Florida.

**[Visual]** Meridian line from Miami straight south; South America slides into view to the right of it.

**Fact 3 — Canada is south of Detroit**
> Number three. From downtown Detroit, if you head south across the river, you land in Canada. The city of Windsor, Ontario sits directly south of Detroit. The Detroit River bends around the city, so the border runs east to west there, and thousands of people cross it every day by bridge and by a tunnel under the river.

**[Visual]** Close-up Detroit River; arrow pointing south from Detroit into Windsor; "CANADA ↓" label.

**Fact 4 — Rome is north of New York**
> Number four. Rome is farther north than New York City. Europe feels warm because the Atlantic Ocean carries heat across from the tropics, not because it's further south. Rome sits at about the same latitude as Chicago, and London is farther north than Calgary in Canada.

**[Visual]** Globe tilts to the Atlantic; latitude line from New York extends east and passes south of Rome.

**Fact 5 — Greenland vs Africa**
> Number five. On most world maps, Greenland looks about the same size as Africa. In reality, Africa is about fourteen times bigger. The map most of us grew up with is called the Mercator projection. It was made in 1569 for sailors because it keeps directions accurate, but the price is that everything near the poles gets blown up.

**[Visual]** Mercator map morphs to the globe; Greenland outline is dragged onto Africa and shrinks to true size.

## CHAPTER 2 — BORDERS THAT MAKE NO SENSE

> Now let's talk about borders, because some of them are completely ridiculous.

**Fact 6 — Russia and the USA**
> Number six. Russia and the United States are just three point eight kilometers apart. Between them sit two islands, Big Diomede and Little Diomede, and the International Date Line runs right between them. They're twenty-one hours apart, so people call them Tomorrow Island and Yesterday Isle.

**[Visual]** Fly to Bering Strait → dive to the islands; date line draws; clocks pop up: "+12" vs "−9".

**Fact 7 — France's twelve time zones**
> Number seven. France has twelve time zones, more than any other country. Thanks to its islands in the Caribbean, the Indian Ocean and the Pacific, the sun never sets on French territory. When it's noon in Paris in winter, it's one in the morning in Tahiti and ten at night in New Caledonia, and all of it is France.

**[Visual]** Globe spin with the 12 territory pings and the time-zone counter (reuse from the Short).

**Fact 8 — The town split in two**
> Number eight. In the town of Baarle, the border between Belgium and the Netherlands zigzags through streets, shops and even living rooms. Some houses are in two countries at once, and your nationality at home depends on where your front door is. There are more than twenty little pieces of Belgium inside the Netherlands there, and even pieces of the Netherlands inside those pieces of Belgium.

**[Visual]** Zoom into Baarle; patchwork of Belgian and Dutch enclaves fills in; border line cuts through a house icon.

**Fact 9 — Point Roberts**
> Number nine. Point Roberts is a small piece of the United States you can't drive to without going through Canada. It sits on the tip of a peninsula below Vancouver, cut off by the border line on the 49th parallel. Its older students cross an international border twice each way just to get to school on the US mainland.

**[Visual]** 49th parallel draws across the peninsula; Point Roberts highlighted; car path loops through Canada.

**Fact 10 — China's single time zone**
> Number ten. China is about as wide as the continental United States, but it uses just one time zone. Cross the border from China into Afghanistan, and you have to turn your clock back three and a half hours. In far western Xinjiang, many people unofficially keep their own local time, two hours behind Beijing, just so their clocks match the sun.

**[Visual]** China lights up in a single colour; the sun rises over the east while the west is still dark; clock jumps at the Afghan border.

## CHAPTER 3 — EARTH IS STRANGER THAN YOU THINK

> Next, the planet itself, which turns out to be really weird.

**Fact 11 — Chimborazo beats Everest**
> Number eleven. The point farthest from Earth's center isn't the top of Mount Everest. It's the top of Chimborazo in Ecuador. The Earth bulges at the equator, so Chimborazo sits about two kilometers farther out into space. Everest is still the highest mountain above sea level, by more than two and a half kilometers, but sea level itself is farther from the center at the equator.

**[Visual]** Equator draws; bulge ellipse; radius lines to Chimborazo and Everest (reuse from the Short).

**Fact 12 — Mauna Kea is the real giant**
> Number twelve. Measured from its base on the ocean floor, Mauna Kea in Hawaii is over ten thousand meters tall. That's more than a kilometer taller than Everest. Most of it is just hidden underwater. Above the waves it reaches about four thousand two hundred meters, high enough that its summit is one of the best places on Earth for telescopes.

**[Visual]** Cross-section: ocean drains away, Mauna Kea rises from the seafloor next to an Everest silhouette.

**Fact 13 — Lake Baikal**
> Number thirteen. Lake Baikal in Siberia is the deepest lake on Earth, more than one thousand six hundred meters deep. It holds about a fifth of all the unfrozen fresh water on the planet's surface. It's also the oldest lake on Earth, around twenty-five million years old, and home to the nerpa, one of the world's only freshwater seals.

**[Visual]** Fly to Baikal; depth gauge drops; "≈ 20% of Earth's surface fresh water" counter.

**Fact 14 — The Dead Sea**
> Number fourteen. The shore of the Dead Sea is the lowest land on Earth, more than four hundred thirty meters below sea level. And the water is so salty you float without even trying.

**[Visual]** Fly to the Dead Sea; elevation gauge drops below zero; "−430 m".

**Fact 15 — The biggest desert is cold**
> Number fifteen. The largest desert in the world isn't the Sahara. It's Antarctica. A desert is defined by how little rain or snow falls, not by heat, and Antarctica barely gets any. It covers about fourteen million square kilometers, roughly one and a half times the size of the Sahara.

**[Visual]** Globe flips to the South Pole; Antarctica glows; Sahara outline dropped on top for size.

## CHAPTER 4 — SIZES THAT BREAK YOUR BRAIN

> Now let's mess with your sense of scale.

**Fact 16 — Africa vs the Moon**
> Number sixteen. Africa is more than twice as wide as the Moon. You could line up two Moons side by side across Africa and still have room to spare. In fact, the United States, China and India all put together would fit inside Africa.

**[Visual]** Africa glows gold; two Moons slide in at true scale (reuse from the Short).

**Fact 17 — Russia vs Pluto**
> Number seventeen. Russia is bigger than Pluto. Russia covers about seventeen million square kilometers, more than the entire surface area of the dwarf planet. And Pluto is a whole world, with mountains of ice and a giant heart-shaped plain you can see in NASA's photos.

**[Visual]** Russia highlighted; Pluto drawn as a sphere and "unwrapped"; size bars compare.

**Fact 18 — Australia vs the Moon**
> Number eighteen. Africa isn't the only one. Australia is wider than the Moon too: about four thousand kilometers from coast to coast, while the Moon is under three thousand five hundred.

**[Visual]** Fly to Australia; one Moon slides across and doesn't cover the width; measurement line.

**Fact 19 — Alaska wins three ways**
> Number nineteen. Alaska is the northernmost, the westernmost and the easternmost state in the US. Its Aleutian Islands stretch so far west that they cross the 180th meridian into the Eastern Hemisphere.

**[Visual]** Aleutian chain lights up island by island and crosses the 180° line; three arrows: N, W, E.

**Fact 20 — Canada's coastline**
> Number twenty. Canada has the longest coastline in the world, more than two hundred thousand kilometers. Walk it at twenty kilometers a day, and it would take you about thirty years. The reason is islands: Canada has tens of thousands of them, many in the frozen Arctic.

**[Visual]** Canada's coastline traces itself in glowing yellow; distance counter ticks up.

## CHAPTER 5 — PLACES THAT SHOULDN'T EXIST

> And finally, places that sound made up.

**Fact 21 — Istanbul**
> Number twenty-one. Istanbul is in two continents at once. The Bosphorus strait splits the city, so half of it is in Europe and half is in Asia. Some people commute between continents every day. It's also been the capital of three empires: the Roman, the Byzantine and the Ottoman.

**[Visual]** Fly to Istanbul; Bosphorus highlighted; "EUROPE | ASIA" labels on either side.

**Fact 22 — The smallest country**
> Number twenty-two. Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, less than half a square kilometer. It's an entire independent country inside the city of Rome.

**[Visual]** Zoom into Rome → Vatican border outlined; size comparison box.

**Fact 23 — The biggest country with no sea**
> Number twenty-three. Kazakhstan is the biggest country in the world without a coastline. It's larger than all of Western Europe, and still has no way to reach the open ocean. Its only coast is on the Caspian Sea, and the Caspian is usually treated as a lake, not an ocean.

**[Visual]** Kazakhstan highlighted; Western Europe outline dropped on top; no-sea icon.

**Fact 24 — Doubly landlocked**
> Number twenty-four. Only two countries are doubly landlocked, meaning they're surrounded only by other landlocked countries. They are Liechtenstein in Europe and Uzbekistan in Central Asia. To reach the ocean from either one, you'd have to cross at least two borders. Liechtenstein sits between Switzerland and Austria, while Uzbekistan is surrounded by five countries, and none of them touch the open ocean.

**[Visual]** Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan pulse; neighbours shade grey; route to the sea crosses two borders.

**Fact 25 — Point Nemo**
> And number twenty-five. Point Nemo, in the South Pacific, is the most remote spot in any ocean. The nearest land is over two thousand six hundred kilometers away. So when the space station passes overhead, at about four hundred kilometers up, the closest humans to Point Nemo are astronauts. It's also where old spacecraft are deliberately brought down, which is why people call it the spacecraft cemetery.

**[Visual]** Pull far out to the empty Pacific; distance rings to the nearest islands; ISS orbit sweeps over; "400 km" vs "2,688 km".
